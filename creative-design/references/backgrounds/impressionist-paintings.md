# Impressionist paintings

- **Look**: visible brushstrokes, light dissolved into dabs of color, soft edges, gardens, water, skies, dusk.
- **Fits**: culture, hospitality, travel, wine and food, wellness, education, brands that want warmth, romance and heritage.
- **Avoid when**: the product is cold, technical or urgent; the painting becomes a pretty but unrelated backdrop.

## Make it

Use **public-domain artwork** from museum open-access programs and verify each item's license.

- Sources: Met Open Access, Art Institute of Chicago, Rijksmuseum, National Gallery of Art, Wikimedia Commons.
- Pick a **detail crop** (a patch of water, sky or foliage) rather than the whole famous painting: it works better as a background and avoids cliche.
- Grade it to the brand: pull a palette from the painting and reuse those colors for UI accents (extract with a palette tool, then define as OKLCH tokens).
- Soften for text: scale up the crop, add a 30-50% overlay in the paper or ink color, or apply a light blur.

Procedural alternative: a canvas "brush" filter that draws many short strokes (6-20px) oriented along the image gradient, colored from the underlying pixel, with random jitter. It gives a painterly look to your own photos.

## Legibility and cost

A single compressed image is cheap. Always add an overlay or reserved quiet zone for text. Credit the artwork and museum in the footer or credits.

## Pairs with

Paper texture, serif type, grainy gradients, cloud sky, blurred photos (a blurred painting is a lovely ambient field).
